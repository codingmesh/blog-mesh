## What the article says

1. The author describes effort as an approximate indication of how much computation a model should spend. Higher settings may also lead to more independent decisions and verification.
2. In the examples, clearer requirements reduce the difference between effort levels. Fast iteration and independent completion call for different allocations.
3. The analysis suggests that more effort can help catch missed edge cases, but it cannot guarantee a correction when the overall direction is wrong. The evaluations have specific operating conditions.

This summarizes the [original article](https://claude.dev/blog/spending-your-effort/). It is not a universal rule across models.

## Applying the idea to an editorial task

Choosing an effort level also means choosing when to receive feedback. Consider an unsettled question about whether a blog homepage should emphasize the author or the writing. Waiting for a fully polished implementation may delay discovering that the chosen structure does not fit the goal.

At that stage, comparing two small, concrete layouts could help decide which better supports the writing. The useful output is enough evidence to make the next decision.

Once the direction is clear, some checks deserve their own budget. After drafting a reading note, for example, review whether each claim is supported by the source, whether a specific experiment has been presented as a universal conclusion, and whether proposed applications are clearly separated from the summary.

One possible workflow divides the work into defining the question, producing something worth discussing, and checking the important risks. Each phase needs a stopping condition. Exploration ends when there is enough information to choose; implementation ends when the agreed behavior works; verification focuses on likely sources of rework or misleading claims. This is an editorial proposal, not a timing experiment.

## What still needs testing

The source's benchmark runs use five attempts per task, and some runs disable production safety interventions. Its failure labels come from an approximate model judge. That setup differs from an everyday project, so its timing and category results should not be transferred directly.

Compare completion time, rework, and missed issues across similar tasks to see whether different allocations help. This note proposes that observation method; it does not claim a measured percentage of time saved.

## What the article says

1. The author reports that the team removed more than 80% of its system prompt for a particular new model without a measured regression on its internal coding evaluations.
2. Instructions spread across system prompts, skills, and project files can duplicate or contradict each other.
3. The article recommends concise project instructions, specialized guidance loaded when needed, and concrete tools and references to communicate requirements.

These are claims and recommendations from the [original article](https://claude.dev/blog/the-new-rules-of-context-engineering-for-claude-5-generation-models/), not findings from an experiment conducted for this note.

## What I take from it

Project instructions tend to grow one incident at a time. A naming mistake adds a rule. An overly broad change adds another. Each addition has a reason, but the collection can lose its boundaries.

I want to know why a rule exists, which failure it addresses, and what would justify removing it. Instructions have a maintenance cost, even when that cost never shows up as a compiler error.

For a blog repository, I would give each requirement a clear home. Stable editorial conventions belong in the project overview. Article structure belongs in a content template. Publishing steps belong in a dedicated guide. The argument of the article being written belongs in the current task. The overview helps readers find the details without repeating them everywhere.

Some rules deserve to stay explicit, including traceable citations and a ban on invented firsthand experience. Editing should make the requirements clearer. A long rule that prevents a recurring mistake may still earn its place.

## What still needs testing

The article describes experience with particular products and models. It does not establish that every model benefits from fewer instructions.

Before adopting a shorter version, I would keep the original and compare both on representative tasks, looking for omissions, misunderstandings, and extra human rework. This note does not include a controlled comparison.
